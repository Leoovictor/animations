# O Grande Cisma — facts and sources

- Latin West (Rome) and Greek East (Constantinople) drifted apart over centuries: language, customs, politics (standard histories; e.g. Britannica, "East-West Schism").
- Authority: Rome claimed the pope's authority over the whole Church; the East saw the bishop of Rome as first in honour among the patriarchs ("first among equals").
- Filioque: the West added "and the Son" (Filioque) to the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed on the procession of the Holy Spirit; the East objected to changing a conciliar text.
- Bread: the Latin Church used unleavened bread (azymes) in the Eucharist; the Greek Church leavened bread — one of the 1054 controversies.
- 16 July 1054: papal legates led by Cardinal Humbert laid a bull of excommunication on the altar of Hagia Sophia against Patriarch Michael Cerularius; Cerularius and his synod excommunicated the legates in return.
- Pope Leo IX, who had sent the legation, died on 19 April 1054, before the bull was laid (the legates' authority is debated for that reason).
- Historians describe the schism as a gradual estrangement, not a single day.
- 1204: the Fourth Crusade sacked Constantinople, a Christian city; the Latin Empire was set up there (1204–1261). This deepened the division.
- 7 December 1965: Pope Paul VI and Patriarch Athenagoras I lifted the mutual excommunications of 1054 (Joint Catholic-Orthodox Declaration).
- Today Catholics and Orthodox remain separate churches.
